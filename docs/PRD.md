# 1. Product Overview

## 1.1 Product Description

A mobile-first financial education and investment learning platform designed for young adults aged 18–30 in Israel.

The platform combines personalized financial education, hands-on learning experiences, and interactive investment simulations to help users develop financial knowledge, understand investment opportunities, and build confidence in financial decision-making.

Unlike traditional educational platforms that rely primarily on reading materials and video lessons, this product follows a **Learn by Doing** approach.

Users learn financial concepts by interacting with simulations, experimenting with different scenarios, making hypothetical investment decisions, and observing the results.

Each user receives a personalized financial learning journey based on their existing knowledge, experience, interests, and learning goals.

The platform focuses on four main educational domains:

- **Personal Finance:** Budgeting, saving, compound interest, inflation, pensions, Keren Hishtalmut, and other Israeli financial products.
- **Stock Market:** Stocks, bonds, ETFs, market indices, diversification, investment strategies, risk management, and long-term investing.
- **Venture Capital:** Startups, startup valuation, funding rounds, venture capital funds, equity ownership, dilution, exits, and investment risks.
- **Cryptocurrency & Blockchain:** Bitcoin, Ethereum, blockchain technology, digital wallets, crypto exchanges, mining, stablecoins, and cryptocurrency risks.

The platform also provides multiple interactive simulation experiences, including:

- Financial concept simulations.
- Historical stock market simulations.
- Live virtual investment portfolios (Paper Trading).
- Cryptocurrency investment simulations.
- Venture capital investment scenarios.

All simulations are educational and hypothetical. They do not involve real-money transactions or guarantee future investment returns.

## 1.2 Product Vision

To make financial education accessible, practical, engaging, and personalized for young adults.

The long-term vision is to create a personal financial learning companion that transforms complex financial concepts into interactive experiences.

Users should be able to progress from having little or no financial knowledge to understanding how financial markets work, evaluating different investment opportunities, recognizing financial risks, and making more informed decisions.

The platform aims to bridge the gap between theoretical financial knowledge and practical understanding through hands-on learning.

## 1.3 Problem Statement

Many young adults enter adulthood without sufficient financial knowledge or practical experience in managing money and understanding investments.

Although financial information is widely available online, it is often fragmented, overly technical, difficult to understand, or not adapted to the individual's level of knowledge.

Common challenges include:

- Limited understanding of basic financial concepts and investment products.
- Difficulty distinguishing between different investment opportunities and their associated risks.
- Lack of accessible explanations about the Israeli financial system, including pensions and Keren Hishtalmut.
- Limited understanding of how the stock market and venture capital ecosystem operate.
- Difficulty understanding cryptocurrencies and blockchain technology.
- Difficulty connecting theoretical financial knowledge to real-world decisions.
- Lack of personalized learning experiences that adapt to individual goals and knowledge levels.
- Lack of opportunities to practice investment decisions without risking real money.
- Fear of making financial mistakes due to insufficient experience.

As a result, many young adults postpone learning about finance or make decisions without fully understanding their implications.

## 1.4 Proposed Solution

The product addresses these challenges through three core capabilities.

### 1. Personalized Financial Guidance

Upon registration, users complete an onboarding questionnaire covering their financial knowledge, experience, interests, and learning goals.

Based on their answers, the platform creates a personalized learning path that adapts to their progress and helps them focus on relevant topics.

### 2. Learn by Doing – Interactive Financial Education

The platform uses hands-on learning as its primary educational method.

Instead of presenting long explanations before users interact with a concept, each learning unit begins with an interactive experience.

Users experiment with financial scenarios, change parameters, observe outcomes, and then receive explanations that help them understand the underlying concepts.

Each learning unit follows this structure:

1. **Experience:** Introduce an interactive simulation or challenge.
2. **Discover:** Allow users to experiment with different parameters and outcomes.
3. **Understand:** Explain the financial concept behind the experience.
4. **Apply:** Present a new scenario where users apply what they learned.
5. **Reflect:** Evaluate understanding through a short challenge or knowledge assessment.

Educational explanations, text, and videos may support the experience, but they should not replace practical interaction.

### 3. Practical Investment Simulations

Users can apply their knowledge through simulations that recreate hypothetical financial scenarios.

Examples include:

- Simulating compound interest through adjustable investment amounts and time periods.
- Exploring the historical performance of real stocks.
- Creating virtual investment portfolios based on market data.
- Tracking virtual investments over time through Paper Trading.
- Exploring cryptocurrency price movements.
- Evaluating hypothetical startup investment opportunities.
- Exploring startup valuations, funding rounds, ownership percentages, and exit scenarios.

All simulations are educational and hypothetical.

## 1.6 Core Value Proposition

A personalized, hands-on financial education experience that bridges the gap between understanding financial concepts and applying them in practice.

The platform enables users to:

- Learn through practical interaction rather than passive reading.
- Learn at their own pace and according to their existing knowledge.
- Explore multiple areas of finance and investment in one place.
- Understand Israeli financial products and financial terminology.
- Experiment with historical market data.

## 1.7 Product Principles

- **Learn by Doing:** Every major learning topic should include an interactive experience.
- **Personalization:** Learning experiences should adapt to each user's knowledge, interests, and goals.
- **Accessibility:** Financial concepts should be explained in clear, simple, and approachable language.
- **Practical Learning:** Users should be able to apply theoretical knowledge through interactive exercises and simulations.
- **Transparency:** Risks, assumptions, fees, and limitations must be clearly communicated.
- **Trustworthiness:** Financial educational content should be accurate, reviewed, and supported by reliable sources.
- **Progressive Learning:** Users should be able to move from foundational concepts to more advanced financial topics at their own pace.

## 1.8 Product Scope and Boundaries

The initial version will focus on financial education, personalized learning, and hypothetical simulations.

The MVP will not:

- Execute real investment transactions.
- Connect to brokerage, banking, pension, or investment accounts.
- Provide live market trading functionality.
- Provide individualized investment recommendations or portfolio management.
- Guarantee investment returns or present hypothetical outcomes as predictions.

The platform is intended for educational purposes and does not replace professional financial or investment advice.

## 3.5 Learn by Doing – Interactive Lesson Engine

The interactive lesson engine is one of the most important product features.

It should provide a reusable structure for creating practical learning experiences across all four educational domains.

### Standard Learning Unit Structure

**Step 1 – Experience**

The user is presented with a simulation, interactive challenge, or real-world scenario.

**Step 2 – Discover**

The user changes parameters, explores different choices, and observes the outcomes.

**Step 3 – Understand**

The application explains the financial concept behind the experience.

**Step 4 – Apply**

The user receives a new scenario and applies the knowledge independently.

**Step 5 – Reflect**

A short assessment checks whether the user understood the concept.

### Requirements

- Lessons should prioritize interactive elements over long text blocks.
- Explanations should be concise and presented at the appropriate learning moment.
- Each interactive lesson should have a clear learning objective.
- Users should receive immediate feedback.
- The platform should track completed learning activities.
- Users should be able to revisit simulations and learning units.
- Different interactive formats should be supported, including calculators, charts, decision-making scenarios, quizzes, and virtual investment exercises.

Not every topic requires a financial market simulation. For example, wallet security may be taught through an interactive security challenge rather than an investment calculator.

## 3.6 Investment Simulator

The Investment Simulator is a core product feature.

It allows users to experience how investing works through hypothetical investments, historical market data, and ongoing virtual portfolios.

The simulator will support multiple investment categories and simulation modes.

### A. Long-Term Investment Simulator

Users should be able to enter:

- Initial investment amount.
- Monthly contribution.
- Investment duration.
- Assumed annual return.
- Inflation assumption.
- Optional fees.

The simulator should display:

- Estimated future value.
- Total amount contributed.
- Estimated investment growth.
- Impact of different return assumptions.
- Nominal and inflation-adjusted outcomes.

The simulator should make it easy to compare different scenarios.

All calculations must clearly communicate their assumptions.

### B. Historical Stock Market Simulation

This feature allows users to select a real publicly traded stock and explore how a hypothetical investment would have performed over a historical period.

**Example User Scenario:**

A user wants to understand what would have happened if they had invested $1,000 in Apple two years ago.

The user should be able to:

1. Search for and select a publicly traded company, such as Apple (AAPL).
2. Enter a hypothetical investment amount.
3. Select a historical investment date.
4. Select an end date or use the latest available market data.
5. View the number of shares or fractional shares that could have been purchased.
6. Explore the investment's historical performance.
7. View the hypothetical portfolio value over time.
8. Understand the difference between the initial investment and its final value.

**Displayed Information:**

- Company name and ticker symbol.
- Historical purchase date.
- Initial investment amount.
- Historical purchase price.
- Number of shares purchased.
- Current or selected end-date value.
- Total profit or loss.
- Percentage return.
- Historical performance chart.

**Additional Capabilities:**

- Compare different historical investment dates.
- Compare different investment amounts.
- Compare multiple stocks.
- Explore historical market events and volatility.
- Include dividends in total-return calculations when reliable data is available.
- Account for stock splits.

The application must clearly distinguish price return from total return, which includes dividends.

Historical simulations must use actual historical market data where available and must not imply that past performance predicts future returns.

### C. Live Paper Trading

This feature allows users to create virtual investments starting from the current date and monitor them over time.

Unlike the historical simulator, Live Paper Trading creates an ongoing virtual portfolio.

**Example User Scenario:**

A user wants to invest $1,000 in Apple today but does not want to risk real money.

The user can create a virtual investment and follow its performance over the following days, weeks, and months.

**User Flow:**

1. Open the Paper Trading section.
2. Search for a publicly traded company.
3. Select a stock.
4. Enter a virtual investment amount.
5. Review the simulated purchase.
6. Confirm the virtual investment.
7. Add the investment to the virtual portfolio.
8. Monitor performance over time.

**Core Features:**

- Virtual starting balance.
- Virtual stock purchases.
- Support for fractional shares in the simulation.
- Virtual cash balance.
- Portfolio overview.
- Profit and loss tracking.
- Percentage return.
- Historical performance chart.
- Transaction history.
- Multiple virtual investments.
- Saved portfolios.
- Ability to create additional virtual portfolios.

**Portfolio Dashboard:**

Users should be able to see:

- Total virtual portfolio value.
- Initial virtual investment amount.
- Current virtual cash balance.
- Total unrealized profit or loss.
- Portfolio performance over time.
- Individual stock performance.
- Portfolio allocation.

**Additional Features:**

- Virtual selling of shares.
- Virtual buying of additional shares.
- Comparison between portfolio holdings.
- Notifications about significant portfolio changes, if enabled.
- Educational explanations of major market movements.

The virtual portfolio should be persistent, allowing users to return to the application and observe how their investments have changed.

### D. Historical Simulation vs. Live Paper Trading

The platform must support both experiences.

| Feature | Historical Simulation | Live Paper Trading |
|---|---|---|
| Starting Point | Selected historical date | Current date |
| Market Data | Historical prices | Latest available market prices |
| Purpose | Learn from past market behavior | Experience ongoing investing |
| Duration | User-selected historical period | Ongoing |
| Portfolio Persistence | Saved scenario | Persistent virtual portfolio |
| Main Learning Outcome | Understand historical returns and volatility | Understand how investments change over time |

Both features should be accessible from the main Simulator section.

### E. Virtual Portfolio Simulator

Users can create hypothetical portfolios using virtual money.

Potential features:

- Explore diversification.

### F. Cryptocurrency Investment Simulator

- View historical price movements.

### G. Venture Capital Simulator

Users can explore hypothetical startup investment scenarios.

Potential features:

- Review a fictional startup profile.
- Examine its business model and funding stage.
- Explore a hypothetical company valuation.
- Calculate ownership percentages.
- Simulate dilution across funding rounds.
- Explore possible exit scenarios.
- Understand potential gains and losses.

The simulator should teach users how venture capital decisions work rather than suggest actual startup investments.

# 6. Technical Architecture

## 6.1 Recommended Technology Stack

| Layer | Technology |
|---|---|
| Mobile Frontend | React Native |
| Framework | Expo |
| Programming Language | TypeScript |
| Backend | Python |
| Backend Framework | FastAPI |
| Database | PostgreSQL |
| Authentication | Secure authentication provider or backend-managed authentication |
| API Communication | REST API |
| Version Control | Git and GitHub |
| Market Data | External financial market data provider |
| Future AI Integration | OpenAI API |

This architecture is intended to support a maintainable MVP while leaving room for future expansion.

## 6.2 High-Level Architecture

The mobile application communicates with the backend through REST APIs.

The backend handles:

- Authentication and authorization.
- User profile management.
- Onboarding responses.
- Learning path generation.
- Course and lesson management.
- Quiz evaluation.
- Simulation calculations.
- Historical market data retrieval and processing.
- Virtual trade execution.
- Virtual portfolio calculations.
- Progress tracking.

PostgreSQL stores user data, educational content, quiz results, virtual transactions, and simulation scenarios.

The frontend is responsible for displaying content, collecting user input, and presenting interactive visualizations.

## 6.3 Market Data Integration

The stock and cryptocurrency simulators require reliable external market data.

The system should support:

- Historical stock prices.
- Latest available stock prices.
- Historical cryptocurrency prices.
- Latest available cryptocurrency prices.
- Corporate actions such as stock splits.
- Dividend information when available.
- Data timestamps and market status.

Market data should be retrieved through a provider whose license permits the intended educational and display use.

The application must clearly indicate whether data is real-time, delayed, or historical.

The backend should normalize data from external providers before it is used in simulation calculations.

API credentials must remain on the backend and must not be exposed in the mobile application.

The system should also handle:

- API rate limits.
- Missing market data.
- Market holidays and trading hours.
- Provider outages.
- Data caching.
- Historical data corrections.

# 8. Simulation Logic and Calculations

## 8.1 Long-Term Investment Calculation

The investment simulator should calculate the hypothetical future value of an initial investment combined with recurring monthly contributions.

For an initial investment and monthly contributions made at the end of each month:

\[
FV = P(1+r_m)^N + M\left(\frac{(1+r_m)^N-1}{r_m}\right)
\]

Where:

- FV = Future Value.
- P = Initial investment.
- M = Monthly contribution.
- r_m = Monthly assumed return.
- N = Total number of months.

For a simplified model using an effective annual return:

\[
r_m = (1+r_a)^{1/12}-1
\]

Where r_a represents the assumed annual return.

The implementation must handle:

- Zero return.
- Zero initial investment.
- Zero monthly contributions.
- Different investment durations.
- Invalid or negative inputs where not supported.

## 8.2 Important Calculation Considerations

The simulator must clearly distinguish between:

- Nominal returns.
- Inflation-adjusted returns.
- Fees.
- Taxes.
- Assumed returns.
- Actual historical returns.
- Price returns.
- Total returns, including dividends where supported.

All displayed results must be labeled as hypothetical estimates where applicable.

## 8.3 Historical Stock Simulation Calculations

The historical stock simulator should calculate how a hypothetical investment would have performed between two selected dates.

The calculation should account for:

- Historical purchase price.
- Historical end-date price.
- Number of shares purchased.
- Fractional shares where supported.
- Stock splits.
- Dividends when included in total-return calculations.
- Currency consistency.
- Market data availability.

Basic price-return calculation:

\[
Return(\%) = \frac{P_{end}-P_{start}}{P_{start}}\times100
\]

Hypothetical investment value:

\[
InvestmentValue = Shares \times P_{end}
\]

The application should distinguish between price return and total return.

Historical results must be based on verified historical data and clearly identify the selected dates and assumptions.

## 8.4 Live Paper Trading Calculations

The Paper Trading engine should simulate virtual transactions without executing real orders.

For each simulated purchase:

- Validate the selected asset.
- Retrieve the latest available permitted market price.
- Calculate the virtual quantity purchased.
- Update the virtual cash balance.
- Create a virtual transaction record.
- Update portfolio holdings.

Portfolio value should be calculated using the latest available market prices.

The system should calculate:

- Total portfolio value.
- Remaining virtual cash.
- Unrealized profit or loss.
- Realized profit or loss.
- Percentage return.
- Asset allocation.
- Portfolio performance over time.

The simulator must clearly distinguish simulated transaction prices from actual executable market prices.

Market data timestamps must be visible to users.

## 8.5 Venture Capital Calculations

The venture capital simulator should support educational calculations such as:

**Ownership Percentage**

\[
Ownership = \frac{Investment}{PostMoneyValuation}
\]

This formula assumes the investment amount and post-money valuation are defined consistently.

**Dilution**

The simulator should show how a founder's or investor's ownership percentage changes after new funding rounds.

**Exit Scenario**

Users should be able to explore hypothetical outcomes based on different exit valuations and ownership percentages.

The simulator must explain that actual venture capital returns depend on additional factors, including investment terms, dilution, liquidation preferences, and the timing of the exit.

## 8.6 Cryptocurrency Simulation Calculations

The cryptocurrency simulator should support hypothetical purchases and portfolio tracking based on cryptocurrency market data.

Calculations should account for:

- Selected cryptocurrency.
- Purchase price.
- Quantity purchased.
- Current or selected end-date price.
- Total investment value.
- Profit or loss.
- Percentage return.
- Relevant transaction assumptions and fees.

The interface must clearly communicate that cryptocurrency markets are volatile and that hypothetical results do not predict future performance.

# 13. Product Success Definition

The product will be successful if it demonstrates that young adults can use a personalized and interactive platform to improve their understanding of finance and investments.

The key differentiators are:

1. **Learn by Doing:** Financial concepts are taught through practical interactive experiences.
2. **Personalization:** Each user receives a learning path based on their knowledge and interests.
3. **Multiple Investment Domains:** Personal Finance, Stock Market, Venture Capital, and Cryptocurrency.
4. **Historical Simulation:** Users can explore how real investments would have performed in the past.
5. **Live Paper Trading:** Users can create virtual investments and monitor their performance over time.
6. **Practical Understanding:** Users can experiment with financial decisions without risking real money.

The ultimate goal is not simply to teach users financial terminology, but to help them understand how financial decisions work, recognize the risks involved, and develop the knowledge and confidence needed to make more informed decisions.
